# Independent experience audit — user-directed reset

Date: 2026-11-15. Reviewed source baseline: `69014f57f00cb313e7c2450cec36bafa6e7f2ef8` (private version 11), the user's supplied screenshots and the existing browser-test source. This is a read-only, evidence-backed product review. It is **not** a new human playtest, a measured fun score, or independent certification. The main agent owns fresh browser observation and implementation.

No `AGENTS.md` was found at the workspace root or in the repository scan. No production files were changed for this review.

## Finding

The user's criticism is supported by the implementation. The game exposes several technically rich simulation laboratories at once, but does not yet organize them into a motivated exploration adventure. Its visual construction assets and direct spacecraft assembly are strengths worth preserving. Passing the existing tests establishes specific mechanics and invariants; it does not establish that a first-time player knows what to do or why.

## What the existing tests actually show

- `tests/browser/mission-flow.spec.ts:54` is named first-time mission and does use catalogue drag through the UI. Its script already knows which card, camera preset and world coordinates to use; `busPoint()` reads internal 3D projection. Other cases call `__game.startMission(..., 'balanced')` or `finishFast()`. These are useful functional tests, not evidence that an unfamiliar player discovers the interaction.
- `tests/browser/transfer-flow.spec.ts` exercises a real solved Earth→Mars mission and pointer edits, but the script retrieves the handle through `getHandleScreen()` and knows the sample-plan recovery controls in advance.
- `tests/browser/transfer-plan-comparison.spec.ts:34` deliberately enters an invalid date and checks exact previous-plan restoration. It correctly tests recovery integrity. It does not check whether the error CTA repairs the invalid input, whether date bounds are discoverable, or whether a first invalid draft retains interactive planets.
- The QA documents already acknowledge the missing external novice study. This new user feedback supplies actual contrary product evidence and should override an assumption of acceptable usability.

## Proposed acceptance criteria for the redesign

These are observable release gates for the revised product, not claims about work already complete.

1. **Situation before specification.** A fresh session first shows a short 3D incident with a visible change in the world, a clear exploration reason and one immediate action. The player can perform a meaningful action without opening a manual or reading a long mission description. Skipping/replaying dialogue does not erase progress or repeat automatically on recalculation.
2. **One exploration world.** Multiple destinations are selectable on the solar-system map. Selecting two different destinations produces genuinely different route endpoints, encounter conditions and discoveries. A completed discovery persists and affects a subsequent mission choice. Destination art alone is insufficient.
3. **Physical path editing.** The player selects a destination in the world, drags an obvious route/encounter control, and sees the candidate curve and arrival marker change before mouse release. Accepted and provisional paths are visually distinct. Release, cancel and undo preserve a coherent state. Right-drag rotates the map and never accidentally edits the path.
4. **No dead-end invalid draft.** Enter/load the user's `2026-11-15` → `2027-02-03` transfer. The world remains visible and operable; the player has a concrete recovery action. One recovery either changes the proposed dates into the supported domain or restores a known valid plan. It must not submit identical invalid inputs again. Show no raw JD range in the normal error UI.
5. **Focused controls.** Fresh assembly has a single primary progression action, the hardware shelf and context-specific edit actions. FR/TOP/RT and all diagnostics are not permanently visible. Selecting a solar panel exposes a power-related interaction; selecting an antenna exposes communications; neither selection is falsely reflected as a selected camera in the shelf.
6. **Assembly remains tangible.** Catalogue drag creates a ghost, a valid/invalid mount signal appears before release, a successful placement produces a visible mounted part and immediate resource change, and an invalid release leaves the craft unchanged. Free rotation and original asset quality remain.
7. **World consequences.** An observation becomes an identifiable discovery (image/spectrum/location/event), not just an incremented number. The player can see captured, transmitted and received states. Mission completion changes the campaign, while failed/partial attempts offer a recoverable next move tied to what happened.
8. **Real browser checks.** Test the complete fresh-entry path with genuine UI pointer/keyboard actions on supported desktop viewport sizes, no scripted state setup to skip the novel sequence. Retain console/network/WebGL checks and save compatibility. Test reduced motion and keyboard focus. Internal read-only telemetry may corroborate a result but must not be the mechanism used to complete the player journey.
9. **Honest usability conclusion.** Independent agents may report heuristic defects. Browser automation may report behavior/performance. Neither can certify fun. Before claiming first-time usability is proven, observe unfamiliar human players explaining their goal and taking the first route-edit action without coaching; retain mistakes and failures as evidence instead of replacing them with a numerical synthetic score.

## Product recommendation

Build the new first session around **notice a situation → choose where to learn more → construct the needed probe → shape a route in the solar map → see a discovery → decide where to go next**. The asteroid incident can start the story, but a narrow imminent-impact premise should not force unrelated outer-planet travel to pretend it is urgent asteroid defense. Let its discovery reveal a broader exploration program. Keep real NASA sources and fictional decisions clearly distinguishable in provenance, without inserting lengthy legal/scientific explanations into every interaction.

The next release should be evaluated against this complete player loop. More isolated laboratory controls or additional explanatory text would not resolve the problem the user described.
