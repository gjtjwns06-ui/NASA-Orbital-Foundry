# An expedition that asks the player to look, decide and bring evidence home

Research and implementation notes, 2026-11-15. This is official-source research and a proposed playable loop, not a claim that the comparison games were personally played or that automated tests establish enjoyment.

## What to borrow from successful games

**Outer Wilds: a tool should reveal something worth investigating.** Mobius describes the Little Scout as a camera, light and hazard probe, the Signalscope as a way to locate mysterious sounds, and worlds whose environments change over time. The useful lesson here is that operating a device changes what the player knows. Our camera should return a framed image of a chosen region, and the next objective should arise from that evidence. A familiar button should serve a visible tool rather than open another settings panel. [Mobius Digital — Outer Wilds](https://www.mobiusdigitalgames.com/outer-wilds.html)

**Elite Dangerous: move from finding a body to investigating it.** Frontier's published getting-started guide distinguishes a broad discovery scan, frequency tuning with a panned reticle, and probes that build surface coverage. The relevant design is layered investigation with immediate spatial feedback. We should keep one spacecraft instrument view and let framing, stability and wavelength change its result; reproducing Elite's many control modes would recreate the clutter the user rejected. [Frontier's guide on its Steam announcements feed, Exploration section](https://store.steampowered.com/news/posts/?appgroupname=Elite%3A+Dangerous&appids=359320&enddate=1760615315&feed=steam_community_announcements)

These are design inferences from official descriptions, not a claim that the resulting controls equal those games' quality.

## Compact playable loop

1. **Approach with a question.** The spacecraft's view reveals a rotating-looking landscape through the changing flyby perspective, with a broad bright region, a darker region and a boundary. They are body-fixed authored regions, not points pasted on the screen. A short incoming line asks what is different, rather than listing three mandatory clicks.
2. **Frame a context image.** The player aims the actual instrument with the mouse and holds/releases the shutter. A wide frame records where the feature sits. The image itself is the reward; a live quality indicator changes with illumination, aim and blur.
3. **Make one consequential investigation.** For a camera mission, narrow the frame onto a chosen feature and track it for detail. For a spectrometer mission, hold that same region through visible and near-infrared samples and compare the resulting plot and uncertainty. A different region in the second band does not complete a valid comparison. The player chooses the region and when the evidence is good enough.
4. **Respond to a condition, then recover.** An announced loss of star-reference quality offers safe recovery at the cost of part of the approach window, or continuing with degraded image stability. Later stabilization removes the measurable smear. Near-Sun heating is a separate continuous condition: stop observing and enter a cooling orientation, or use the remaining safe time for a shorter exposure. Autonomous safing protects the craft and existing records; there is no random destruction roll.
5. **Send the evidence, then receive it.** Downlink drains stored bits according to a physical transmission rate. The picture is not recorded as received at Earth until the separate light-time has passed in the accelerated presentation. The journal keeps the player's image and measured comparison, and uses it to pose the next question.

The ordinary display needs a shutter, a contextual command when a condition matters, and a view switch. Wavelength selection belongs beside the active spectrometer; recovery actions belong beside the active warning. Camera motion and the scene provide feedback before the player has to read a paragraph.

## Scientific reasons for these interactions

- Precise attitude is needed for science pointing and high-gain communication. NASA also distinguishes spacecraft attitude from its freefall trajectory: turning the craft does not steer its path unless thrust is applied. Our mouse aim changes observation direction, not the heliocentric route. [NASA — Onboard Systems](https://science.nasa.gov/learn/basics-of-space-flight/chapter11-2/)
- Exposure, motion and data quality are linked. The New Horizons LORRI archive explicitly notes that scan imaging needs sufficiently short exposure to avoid substantial point smear. The implementation therefore integrates signal and residual angular tracking motion; a quick click or an empty-space ray cannot earn a science result. [NASA PDS — LORRI Arrokoth raw dataset](https://pds.nasa.gov/ds-view/pds/viewProfile.jsp?dsid=NH-A-LORRI-2-KEM1-V6.0)
- Spectrometers measure intensity by wavelength. Comparing two synthetic reflectance bands is an understandable first approximation, but two values do not identify a chemical or prove the presence of water. The UI must label them as an illustrative two-band comparison, with uncertainty. [NASA — Science Instruments](https://science.nasa.gov/learn/basics-of-space-flight/chapter12-1/)
- Safe mode preserves power, thermal safety and commandability so operators can recover. It should suspend an opportunity and invite recovery, rather than punish the player with arbitrary instant failure. [NASA — Safe Mode engineering guidance](https://swehb.nasa.gov/pages/viewpage.action?pageId=133235173)
- Real deep-space operations use prepared onboard sequences, account for one-way light-time and compare planned events with telemetry. Direct mouse aim is an onboard execution/rehearsal view; it is not represented as instantaneous Earth control. A short onboard command acknowledgement and physical Earth light-time are separate quantities. [NASA — Cruise operations](https://science.nasa.gov/learn/basics-of-space-flight/chapter15-1/)

## Implemented domain and its limits

`src/solar/encounter.ts` provides an independent, analytic two-body hyperbola at the cruise arrival speed. Position and velocity conserve energy and angular momentum. Its authored periapsis and encounter plane bridge from the planet-centre cruise endpoint; this is **not** a continuous, high-fidelity patched-conic arrival or an inherited GMAT validation. It reuses the existing model's mean radii and gravitational parameters. The sphere, frozen body frame, sample regions, local Sun orientation and feature reflectances are explicit approximations. Rings, atmosphere, moons, actual terrain and body spin are not dynamically resolved.

The local window spans six body radii and is presented in 150 play seconds. This may slow down the tiny asteroid's fast flyby. During shutter hold, physical progression slows to at most one physical second per play second. This time manipulation must be visible in the instrument presentation; a player must not interpret it as a real live-feed timing model.

`src/solar/operations.ts` accepts the rendered aperture position and boresight, ray-intersects the body's surface, identifies an actual nearby sample region, and evaluates illumination, foreshortening, footprint and pixel scale. Narrow framing is a crop: the hardware's native IFOV is a resolution floor, so digital zoom does not manufacture spatial detail. Signal calibration is authored, not a measured LORRI or commercial detector specification. Synthetic band values and repeatable noise support an illustrative comparison rather than real chemical retrieval.

The camera's evidence requirement is a context frame and a detailed region. Spectroscopy needs valid visible and near-infrared records of the same region. Valid records preserve SNR, accumulated smear, footprint, GSD, exposure and data size. Low-quality attempts remain previews; they do not silently erase prior results or fill the recorder. Losing the surface or changing the region/band clears the in-progress exposure, so a later empty photograph cannot accompany earlier valid measurements. Reacquisition starts a fresh exposure. At the end of the finite observation window, new captures stop while existing records remain available for transmission.

Battery capacity, storage capacity, sensor power and available optical parameters come from the existing design. Energy uses watts × physical seconds / 3,600. The existing authored carrier still provides 300 W; a starter drawing about 100 W should not suffer a fabricated battery emergency after a few seconds. Heat uses a disclosed lumped training model with a 5 kJ/K heat capacity, passive conductance and an inverse-square absorbed solar load. Installed radiator count improves the assumed conductance. This is not thermal certification.

`transmittedBits` means bits sent by the spacecraft. The controller must apply Earth light-time before granting a journal receipt. Checkpoint restoration rebuilds geometry and calibration from the current design, bounds numerical values, validates acquired records and stops an interrupted held exposure. Existing campaign and discovery save fields are left to the controller's backward-compatible persistence adapter.

## Verification and the next question

The initial domain suite checks all eight visitable destinations at an 8 km/s arrival speed using actual aperture rays; camera context/detail or a same-region spectral pair is reachable. It also checks hyperbolic invariants, empty-space rejection, the native-resolution floor, differing spectral measurements with uncertainty, smear and recovery after the forecast choice, thermal cooling without record deletion, physical downlink independent of asteroid playback, and malformed checkpoint rejection.

These are mathematical and functional checks. Actual rendered-aperture browser tests still need to show that a player can find a region, predict why a photograph is blurred, recover from an event and receive the image. A human playtest must then determine whether framing and investigating the second world feels worth doing again.
