# Experience reset: independent final advisory

Reviewed 2026-11-15. Scope: the revised Solar Expedition entry, assembly handoff, route interaction, encounter and journal. This is a source, artifact and model review; it is not a human novice study, a fun score, or certification of the full commercial-game ambition.

## Verdict

**No additional release-blocking defect found in the reviewed redesign preview.** The revised presentation is substantially less crowded, and the tested first expedition has a complete playable outcome. This verdict is limited to the inspected desktop browser evidence and the checks below. It does not establish that the longer campaign remains engaging, that every browser works, or that the full project goal is complete.

## Evidence inspected

- `test-results/.last-run.json`: passed, no failed test IDs. This reviewer inspected the recorded result rather than independently rerunning the whole browser suite.
- `tests/browser/artifacts/solar-expedition/journey-result.json`: Edge 157.0.4341.46, 1440 × 900, no recorded page errors. The corresponding test source uses real pointer operations for the route and three observations, then transmission, a different destination, reload and a retained journal entry.
- Final 21:06–21:07 artifacts: `assembly-context.png`, `aster-observations.png`, `first-discovery.png`, and `neptune-1280.png`. No blocking overlap or clipped primary action was visible in these four images. The latter is 1280 × 720.
- Read `src/solar-experience.ts`, the campaign handoff and assembly markup in `src/main.ts`, `src/solar/campaign.ts`, the scene interaction/lifecycle points, and both new browser test files.
- Independently executed the model's departure search (0–720 days from the campaign epoch in 60-day steps) for all eight visitable destinations, using each destination's required sensor. Every destination produced a fuel-feasible route. This was a read-only model check, not eight completed browser expeditions.

## Findings supporting the verdict

1. **The first screen has a coherent invitation.** A short three-beat incident now leads to the asteroid expedition, and `introSeen` persists. This replaces the former long first mission brief as the main entry. `SolarExperience.nextBeat()` and the native reload test support the once-completed story behavior.
2. **Assembly selection now means selection.** The reviewed workshop image has no permanently highlighted optical-camera shelf card and no central five-button inspection bar. The assembly browser test actually places a camera, selects an engine, and checks that the camera loses its selected state. Precision views remain available through a drawer; ordinary operation retains right-drag and one visible overview control.
3. **Route manipulation gives immediate feedback.** `pointerMove()` recalculates the route while dragging, updates the date/fuel summary and projects the handles. The production journey captures and asserts a changed arrival date before release. `pointerUp()` explicitly processes the final unthrottled coordinate. The old raw-JD/error/retry loop is not the main campaign interaction.
4. **An initially expensive route is recoverable.** The Neptune screenshot shows an infeasible initial route, but its visible departure-search action has a meaningful solution: departure +180 days, flight 5,190 days, about 3,116 kg propellant against 6,500 kg capacity in the independent check. The maximum fuel among the eight searched, correctly equipped starter craft was this Neptune route. `findWindow()` replaces the plan and prevents unchanged repeated search buttons until the player edits the route.
5. **Equipment affects readiness.** Destination-specific camera/spectrometer requirements, active power, battery reserve, cost, mass and propellant are checked before launch. The campaign tests include valid hardware placements where moving an obstructing panel changes camera or antenna clearance without changing mass. This is an actual assembly consequence, not only a label change.
6. **The first expedition ends in an artifact.** The three pointer observations enable transmission; the image and discovery are retained in the journal. The final report image has readable actions and the journal survives reload. Saved image and receipt-date inputs are bounded before rendering.
7. **Claims about the simulation are bounded in-product.** The model panel distinguishes approximate planetary motion and Lambert routing from authored close-up observations, carrier resources and reception. It explicitly excludes inherited GMAT validation, landing and multi-flyby analysis.

## Non-blocking follow-up risks

- **The encounter mechanic still repeats three screen-space placements.** Profiles change instruments, labels and the next question, but the physical action remains the same. `collect()` counts successful authored target placements, not measured surface features or spectra. A human playtest is still needed to determine whether several destinations feel meaningfully different. Do not present this test result as proof of fun or as eight distinct science simulations.
- **This is a set of Earth-origin expeditions, not a continuous grand tour.** Each new route is calculated from Earth. There is no new campaign-level multi-leg swingby chain or persistent en-route fuel consumption. The model panel correctly states this limit; release descriptions should do the same.
- **Some automated claims are narrower than their labels.** The main journey enters and returns from the craft editor, but does not mutate the craft and assert every placement after that round trip. A separate assembly test verifies actual part placement and selection. Source review supports clone-based handoff, but an edited-craft/reload identity assertion would strengthen future regression coverage. Likewise, the journey comment mentions Escape cancellation while the test actually exercises keyboard editing and Undo, not Escape.
- **Initial route cost can be visually extreme.** Neptune's default route displays roughly 4.8 million kg. The recovery action works, so this is not a dead end; nevertheless, an initially feasible first suggestion or a clearer contrast between the current attempt and a feasible opportunity could make distant exploration less discouraging. Preserve direct route editing and visible tradeoffs.
- **Desktop evidence is bounded.** The reviewed evidence covers Edge at 1440 × 900 and 1280 × 720. It does not demonstrate touch-first use, screen-reader completion, low-end-GPU performance, other engines, or comprehension by people unfamiliar with the game.

Recommended next evaluation: observe several new players completing the asteroid expedition and one sensor-changing destination without coaching. Record where they hesitate, whether they can predict a drag's effect, and whether they voluntarily attempt another world. Those observations should guide the next gameplay iteration; automated success alone cannot settle them.

Release recorder follow-up: the final combined 8-case native suite subsequently passed with no skipped, unexpected or flaky cases (116.808 seconds). The original report is `docs/qa/experience-reset-final-browser.json`. This is a recorded root execution result, not an additional independent human review.

## Post-review addendum — focused control regression and Venus expedition

Added 2026-11-15 after reviewing further artifacts and the corresponding source. The original review above is preserved. This addendum updates two of its test-coverage caveats; the final combined eight-case browser run was still pending when this addendum was written, so no combined-run pass is asserted here.

**Escape coverage found a real defect, then verified its fix.** `docs/qa/experience-reset-controls-run-r1.json` records one failed native-control test: after a mouse route drag with focus on `BODY`, Escape left the arrival date at 2027-11-05 and propellant at 1,552 kg instead of restoring 2028-03-17 and 932 kg. The prior HUD-scoped key listener could miss that keyboard event. The source now registers the listener on `window` for the active experience and removes it in `dispose()`. Route/scan cancellation also clears its state and releases pointer capture. The unchanged focused test in `experience-reset-controls-run-r2.json` passed 1/1 with no errors: Escape restored departure, arrival and propellant before mouse-up, a new mouse drag worked afterward, and Undo restored the original route. Both runs retain native `BODY` focus evidence. This was an actual functional correction, not merely additional proof of the earlier implementation.

**The changed-equipment handoff now has an executed destination journey.** `tests/browser/artifacts/solar-second-destination/venus-journey-result.json` and its test source show Venus blocked for a missing spectrometer, actual shelf keyboard placement of a new spectrometer on the free `nx` face, return to the map with launch enabled, three native pointer observations, transmission and a received Venus journal entry. The saved receipt contains the newly installed spectrometer alongside the six original components and reports a captured image. The inspected `venus-received-journal.png` displays the Venus encounter image and the returned discovery. No page errors were recorded on Edge 157 at 1440 × 900.

This materially narrows the original craft-handoff coverage gap: a newly fitted instrument survives the handoff and is usable by the mission. It is a separate Venus run from a fresh campaign, not proof of a completed eight-world playthrough or of consecutive asteroid-to-Venus discovery persistence in one run. The test retains part IDs, kinds and faces; it is not an exhaustive equality assertion over every possible placement field. Human comprehension and sustained enjoyment remain unevaluated.
