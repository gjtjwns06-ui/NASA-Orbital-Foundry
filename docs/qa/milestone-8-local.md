These results do not imply complete original-specification acceptance or commercial-game parity.

## Player experience

The new **First Light** mission begins with a compact satellite whose cable guide obstructs a solar wing between its folded and deployed endpoints. The player can inspect the actual intermediate collision, move the guide directly on the 3D mounting surface, and drag the onboard deployment and downlink schedule. The mission requires 90 W and stable pointing for 60 seconds, then 16 Mbit of commissioning diagnostics received on Earth within three hours. A larger fixed-wing design offers a viable alternative with a different fairing requirement.

Actual hinge motion, motor work, angular momentum, electrical/RF contacts, shadows, battery supply and signal arrival determine the outcome. An editor preview cannot move the launched mechanism. The report is commissioning telemetry, not a scientific image. The model and its explicit approximations are described in [deployment-model.md](../deployment-model.md).

The commissioning workshop has its own save slot. A confirmed pause barrier captures explicit saves; unreadable local data remains available for original-file backup. Failed or cancelled imports retain the accepted flight. If power fails with a signal already travelling, reception can continue without restoring the spacecraft or granting additional powered work.

Keyboard assembly is also available in the existing Earth/Mars hardware builder. This adds an alternative to direct mouse mounting; it does not replace mouse manipulation.

## Visual and direct-interaction review

The latest [actual T+1:14 Edge view](../../tests/browser/artifacts/deployment-trail-native/m8-trail-edge-r1-closure/actual-one-times-advanced-retained-trail.png) and the [completed working-face view](../../tests/browser/artifacts/deployment-v8b/m8-v8b-edge-r1/edge-terminal-working-faces.png) show working3D hardware, readable solar leaves and a curved Earth horizon. They also show why the requested polished Mars Horizon-style presentation is still a target rather than an achieved equivalence:

- The default craft silhouette often reads as an open gold box: broad flat walls and a pale interior dominate, while the five distinct internal equipment families are tiny or hidden at this distance. The next art pass should improve edge definition, metal/foil/solar contrast and selected-component framing inside the actual physical envelopes. More unseen detail or additional texture count would not solve that first-view readability problem.
- Close Earth geography fills much of the frame and remains visibly soft. The explicit working-face view improves which surfaces can be seen, but does not by itself produce a consistently composed hero shot. Keep the real planet geometry; give orbital overview and hardware inspection distinct compositions, with a readable limb/background and lighting that separates hinges and panels from Earth.
- The mission goal is present, but its live progress is distributed through a full-height telemetry column; the opposite column repeats long model identifiers. At720p this feels like an engineering dashboard. A stronger play view would foreground the current90W→60s→16Mbit objective and active mechanism, reveal technical identifiers on demand, and give more screen area to the part being manipulated. These are proposed presentation changes, not already implemented behavior.
- The real guide-drag→clearance→unfolding→Earth receipt chain is the strongest interactive loop. Its first actionable part is still found through a text card and a focus button. A spatial cue on the actual offending guide, a more prominent collision/sweep preview, and feedback at the exact moment a valid placement clears the obstruction would make the direct manipulation easier to discover. The completion card should make the player's repair and its measured cost/energy tradeoff visible, instead of leaving the reward chiefly as a telemetry total. Broad mission variety and consequential in-flight decisions remain separate content work; this single commissioning loop does not yet provide the pacing of the user's reference games.
