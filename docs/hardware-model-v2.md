# Hardware profile 2.0.0

This is a separate calculation profile. Legacy `Design` records without `hardwareVersion` retain model 1.3.0, its nine kinds, fixed bus, original geometry and all original runtime arithmetic. Legacy Journey saves retain transfer-1.0.0. Actual pre-change checkpoints are pinned in `tests/fixtures/hardware-legacy-v2.json`; replay tests compare their entire runtime and all existing result fields. New runs identify hardware-2.0.0, sim 2.0.0 or transfer-2.0.0, and a SHA-256 catalog identity. Unknown/mixed versions or catalog hashes are rejected. Hardware and legacy autosaves use separate IndexedDB keys. Upgrading a draft is a UI action; an old flight is never silently upgraded.

## What is functional

Two original buses share a 1.3 × 1.6 × 1.3 m external envelope but differ in mass, protected internal bay, fuel, EPS rails, simultaneous power, MPPT inputs and compatible data ports. Sixteen model IDs resolve to the same definitions used by evaluation, renderer, ray sampling and runtime. External objects use actual oriented proxy boxes; internal carriers must fit their selected bay. Fixed solar has three panel apertures, the six-wing assembly has eighteen; each uses five weighted rays, cosine incidence, eclipse and inverse-square solar flux. Their support rails and dish feed stem belong to the shared geometry. Aperture rays test the bus and other parts; self-obstruction within a single articulated part remains a disclosed approximation. No renderer scale changes substitute for physical geometry.

The connection solver allocates exact voltage-compatible rails and interface/driver-compatible data ports by deterministic matching. Unsupported voltage, insufficient peak port capacity, oversubscribed simultaneous power, missing interfaces, port shortage and excess MPPT inputs block launch. Physical-signature sorting makes cosmetic part IDs/order/rotation by full turns irrelevant to automatic selection. The chosen radio and a band-compatible passive antenna form one active RF chain; other radios retain idle power. One science instrument and one ADCS are supported. Multiple optical devices explicitly block launch, preventing their fields of view, resolution and data volume from being combined.

The UHF chain has a crossed-dipole pattern and low payload rate. X band uses a 1 m authored reflector and a narrow continuous gain pattern. Link equations include actual RF output, frequency, range loss, orientation loss, source-specific signal modes, assumed coding threshold, framing fraction, receiver gain and temperature. The payload rate is capped by the connected radio port and recorder read rate. Passive antennas consume no DC power. Real uplink frequency coordination, vendor firmware, receiver tracking, antenna polarization dynamics and DSN scheduling are outside this educational model.

Instrument exposure must remain valid before readout through its actual payload data port. A reserved frame buffer prevents overflow; unfinished exposure is discarded if pointing/illumination/quality is lost, while existing science data is preserved. Optical assumptions are 24° full field / 1024 pixels / 10 bits per pixel for the wide camera and 8° full field / 4096 pixels / 12 bits per pixel for the precision camera. IFOV is field angle divided by pixel count; the same selected sensor drives geometric footprint, GSD, rays and frame size. Daylight exposures are authored 5 ms (wide) and 1 ms (precision), not vendor claims. These are authored cameras, not named NASA hardware. Wide frames are 10,485,760 bits; precision frames are 201,326,592 bits. The precision camera's larger frame and tighter field are genuine constraints, not an automatic score bonus. Hardware precision explicitly requests a radius-10-km region; legacy precision retains its radius-70-km objective. The precision detector supports authored electronic region-of-interest window readout: 1024² × 12 bits for precision and 512² × 12 bits for survey. The window can be centered on the chosen sample only inside the original optical field; it cannot see outside that field. IFOV and pixel bit depth are unchanged, and no compression or quality exemption is applied. Precision accepts the crop only when its minimum projected width (slant range × IFOV × window pixel count) covers the full 20 km diameter, while worst-axis GSD separately satisfies the 35 m limit. A sharp but physically undersized crop is rejected. Survey samples a local patch at each existing 4-degree grid-cell center; it does not claim the cropped image covers the whole 4-degree cell. Corridor imaging retains the full 4096²-pixel frame. Detector noise, smear compensation, compression and synthetic image generation are not sensor validation.

The authored spectrometer produces 1,600,000 bits per exposure (200 spectral channels × 500 spatial samples × 16 bits), with a 15 s exposure. M03 legacy remains 24 Mbit per spectrum; only hardware-profile runs use the hardware definition. Storage capacities are bits, rates bits/s, electrical load W and battery energy Wh. BPX recommended usable energy is 86 Wh per pack, not its 100 Wh nominal label. EPS charge/discharge limits, conversion efficiency and battery thermal/idle policy are explicitly authored. Bus battery protection and MPPT converters are integrated assumptions; raw cell thermal/electrical transients are not modeled.

ADCS published torque and momentum are converted to angular acceleration and rate using uniformly distributed proxy-box mass, rotated self inertia and the parallel-axis theorem about combined spacecraft CG. The controller uses the largest body-diagonal inertia as a disclosed scalar approximation; this is not the maximum principal eigenvalue when off-diagonal terms are nonzero. This is a bounded quaternion pursuit controller, not full Euler rigid-body dynamics or vendor flight software: it omits axis-coupled angular momentum, disturbance torques, flexible arrays, star-tracker availability, momentum desaturation and wheel saturation history. Reaction-wheel power is drawn while slewing. Safe mode turns off payloads and radios but retains ADCS and pack management, plus an authored half-duty CPU base load; it does not receive free attitude control. Hardware position and bus mass therefore change actual acquisition and communication opportunities. Propulsive burns remain ideal impulsive maneuvers; these attitude constraints apply to observation/communication/solar pointing and do not certify engine alignment.

## Presets and evidence

M01 has successful L18 and D35 layouts using a wide camera, UHF chain and XACT50; observation begins at injection and ends at 1,800 s, followed by downlink. Initial M01 attitude is the first scheduled attitude, an explicit injection assumption. The weaker XACT15 is supported but loses moving-target opportunities on this large vehicle. Two additional narrow-camera examples are exported by `createHardwareRegionDesign`: precision at 800 km / 51.6° returns one 12,582,912-bit ROI frame; survey at 1,800 km / 40° returns four distinct 3,145,728-bit windows. Both use actual UHF transmission, ground access and finite attitude. Replacing their precision sensor with the wide sensor fails the actual GSD requirement. M02 balanced and science layouts use Iris, a reflector, spectrometer and one or two BPX packs. Both return at least three distinct packets under actual transmission and light-time rules.

M03 uses `createHardwareTransferDesign` and `createHardwareTransferPlan` for new presets. The balanced route requires a dual-pack bank and charging between downlinks because Earth pointing reduces solar input. Its 90-minute observation is followed by 1-hour transmissions separated by 2-hour charging windows; the science route can sustain its original schedule with two packs. Imported custom craft and operations are not automatically repaired. Hardware Journey budget is 760 game credits, legacy is 680. No credit amount is a vendor or launcher price. Two success layouts, resource-grid convergence, genuine packet delivery, source arithmetic, failed compatibility, acquisition time, low ADCS performance and separate-profile save replay are covered by hardware-domain/transfer/audit tests.

The public machine-readable source card now includes TSP, BPX, AX100, Iris and XACT references. Manufacturer values remain qualified by revision, configuration and temperature. Carrier masses, rail tolerances, data drivers, graphics, receiver network and mission costs are original assumptions. Iris brochure inconsistencies are disclosed instead of mixed into one optimistic figure. The ISISPACE patch remains reference-only. Catalog content is published in `public/data/hardware-catalog-v2.json`; its SHA-256 hashes UTF-8 `JSON.stringify(content)` with stored property order. Model source files and tests pin the same identity. Manufacturer images/CAD/PDFs are not redistributed as permissively licensed assets; all meshes are original procedural work.

GMAT evidence continues to validate only the documented central-gravity trajectories. It does not validate this hardware model, manufacturer performance, RF reliability, thermal margins or spacecraft flightworthiness. Source URLs and exact revisions are in `public/data/component-sources.json` and the earlier `hardware-expansion-proposal.md`.
